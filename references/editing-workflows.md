# Editing workflows

## Brief to review

1. Gather the deliverable, audience, purpose, platform, runtime, source assets, and constraints.
2. Write the core takeaway and a beat outline. A short should start with a specific hook, deliver one coherent idea, and close with a fitting action or resolution.
3. Map each beat to a scene. For each scene record a time or word range, visual, purpose, transition, caption treatment, and audio cue if needed.
4. Build a rough cut before decorative motion. Check the story without effects, then add only visuals that clarify or emphasize.
5. Review a draft at normal speed and on a phone-sized view. Fix confusing cuts, rushed text, repeated scenes, and unsafe placement.
6. Confirm rights, export settings, and audio; render and review the final file.

## Scene-by-scene plan

Use `director/templates/brief.example.json` and `director/templates/edl.example.json` as schema-shaped examples. For scripted voice-over with timing data, anchor scene and animation changes to spoken words. With footage-first edits, use source timecodes and preserve handles for transitions. Record uncertain timing as an estimate and review it manually.

Typical visual choices include A-roll, B-roll, screen recordings, product captures, still images, typography, diagrams, or a deliberately quiet shot. Use the real interface or footage when a claim depends on it; never invent product evidence. Avoid stacking a transition, text animation, sound effect, and camera move on every cut.

## Pacing and transitions

Let the information density, delivery, and emotion determine shot length. Change visual information when a new beat begins; hold longer for a complex explanation or emotional reveal. Use cuts for continuity and pace, dissolves for time or mood shifts, and motivated motion transitions when direction or shape carries across shots. Keep transitions short enough that the viewer does not lose the next spoken point.

## Format adaptations

- Vertical short: keep the opening immediately legible, reserve platform UI areas, use larger captions, and make each beat understandable on a phone.
- Long-form: build sections and visual resets around chapters, use lower-third conventions consistently, and protect room for detailed explanations.
- Square or landscape: recompose scenes rather than simply cropping vertical layouts; verify titles and subjects remain within frame.
