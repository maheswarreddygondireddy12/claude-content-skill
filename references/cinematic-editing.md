# Cinematic editing

Cinematic style comes from intentional framing, rhythm, light, sound, and continuity. It does not require a particular LUT, transition, or aspect ratio.

- Establish geography and screen direction before breaking continuity for effect.
- Use shot size and camera movement to guide attention. Avoid movement without a story purpose.
- Shape rhythm with contrast: a held image can make the next cut feel stronger than another fast transition.
- Use motivated cuts on action, dialogue, sound, or a clear change in idea.
- Treat titles and graphics as part of the composition. Preserve negative space and visual hierarchy.
- Review the sequence in context; a dramatic shot may not support the story when isolated.

For a cinematic short, consider a clean opening image, controlled contrast, restrained transitions, selective sound design, and a deliberate final beat. Adapt these choices to the source material and accessibility needs.
